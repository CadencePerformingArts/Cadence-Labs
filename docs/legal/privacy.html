Privacy Policy

Draft · last edited August 2026 · not yet in effect

The short version

  • No advertising, ever.
  • No sale or sharing of personal data with data brokers or advertisers, ever.
  • No third-party trackers, analytics scripts, or tracking pixels on any Cadence page. The only analytics are Cadence's own, stored in Cadence's own database.
  • You can read every public scoreboard without an account and without being individually identified.

What Cadence collects, and why

  • Account email address. Collected when you create an account. Used to sign you in (see below), to attach your workspace memberships to you, and to deliver notifications your organization sends. Cadence accounts are optional for scoreboards and required for Ensemble workspaces.
  • First-party usage analytics. Cadence records simple usage events (a page view, an action, a session identifier) in its own database to understand what is used and what is broken. There is no third-party analytics service involved. When you are signed in, the event's user identity is stamped server-side by the database from your authenticated session — the browser cannot claim to be someone else, and signed-out events carry no user identity at all.
  • Workspace content. Whatever your organization and its members put into their workspace: posts, messages, calendar events, attendance, files, form responses (which can include medical information the organization requests), and drill. Cadence stores and serves this content on the organization's behalf; the organization controls who in the workspace can see what.

Cadence collects nothing else. There is no location tracking, no contact upload, no behavioral profiling.

Sign-in without passwords

Cadence uses magic-link email authentication: to sign in, you receive a one-time link or code at your email address. Cadence never stores a password, so a password of yours can never leak from Cadence.

Where data lives

Account data, workspace content, and analytics are stored in Supabase (a hosted Postgres database and file-storage service). Two properties of that setup matter for privacy:

  • Row-level security. Who can read which row of workspace data is enforced by rules inside the database itself, keyed to the signed-in user and the organization's settings — not by application code deciding what to show. Sensitive categories, such as medical form responses, have deliberately narrow rules (the submitter, their linked guardians, and staff the organization has granted access).
  • Private file storage. Uploaded files live in a private storage bucket with no public URLs. Files are served only through short-lived signed URLs minted for a signed-in member whose database permissions allow them to see that file.

Retention

  • Workspace content is kept for the life of the workspace. If a trial or subscription lapses, the workspace becomes read-only but its content is preserved — nothing is deleted because of a billing state.
  • Analytics events are operational data and may be aggregated or pruned over time; they are never enriched with outside data.
  • Deleted content and deleted accounts pass through a recovery period before being permanently removed, as described on the data practices page.

Export and deletion

Organizations can request an export of their workspace data, and any person can request deletion of their account and personal data, via the contact address published on the Cadence site. Deletion requests are honored with a recovery period to protect against mistakes and account takeover. Details are on the data practices page.

Children's data

Minors use Cadence Ensemble only through an organization that invites them — Cadence does not market to children or offer them standalone accounts. The design intent for minors' data is:

  • Data minimization. Cadence itself requires only an email address; everything else about a young member exists because their organization chose to collect it for its own operations.
  • Guardian involvement. Organizations link parents and guardians to their own students in the database, so a guardian can see their own child's information — including form responses — and nothing else.
  • Organization-controlled access. Who may contact or see information about a minor is governed by the organization's settings and enforced by database-level permission rules, described on the youth safety page.

Cadence does not claim compliance with any children's-privacy or education-privacy law. Formal legal review of how these laws apply to Cadence, and any changes that review requires, is a mandatory step before launch and before any organization is charged.

Medical information

Organizations may use Cadence forms to collect medical information (allergies, medications, emergency contacts) from their members. Cadence's design intent is that this data is visible only to the submitter, their linked guardians, and the staff roles the organization explicitly grants access — enforced in the database, not just the interface. Cadence does not claim compliance with any health-privacy law; formal review is a required pre-launch step.

Who can see your data

  • Your organization's members, according to the roles and settings your organization configures.
  • Cadence's operator, only as needed to run the service, investigate abuse, or comply with law.
  • Supabase, as the infrastructure provider hosting the database and files.
  • No one else. There are no advertisers, brokers, or "partners".

Changes and contact

This draft may change without notice until final terms take effect; material changes after that will be announced to workspace administrators. Privacy questions and requests go to the contact address published on the Cadence site.
