Youth Safety & Organization Responsibility

Draft · last edited August 2026 · not yet in effect

Cadence Ensemble is built for marching-arts programs, and most programs include minors. This page describes the safety mechanics that actually exist in the product today — enforced by the database, not by hiding buttons — and where responsibility sits between the organization and Cadence.

Who is responsible for what

  • The organization controls its workspace: who is invited, who is marked as a minor, which adults hold staff roles, which safety settings are on, and how reports are handled. For its workspace data, the organization is the controller. Organizations remain responsible for following their own youth-protection obligations — school district policies, circuit rules, and applicable law.
  • Cadence is the platform. It provides the enforcement machinery described below, keeps the safety rules on by default, and stores the organization's data under the organization's settings. Cadence does not run background checks on anyone, does not verify who is an adult, and does not proactively monitor private conversations.

How messaging restrictions actually work

The rules below are enforced by row-level security and triggers inside the database. A modified app, a script, or a direct API call hits the same wall as the official interface.

Private conversations involving minors are off by default

  • Each organization has two settings: student_dms (may two minors hold a private conversation) and student_staff_dms (may a minor and a staff member hold one). Both default to off; an organization must deliberately turn them on.
  • When either person in a would-be private conversation is a minor, the database checks the relevant setting before anyone can be added to the conversation. Two adults may always message each other.
  • Only members whose role is owner or staff count as supervised adults for the staff rule. Parents, boosters, and volunteers deliberately do not — a volunteer adult does not get a private channel to a student just by being an adult in the workspace.

The rules follow the people, not the label

  • The pair rules apply to any two-person conversation created by an ordinary member, whatever it is called. Renaming a direct message a "group chat" does not exempt it: conversations not created by someone holding the staff-level chat-creation permission are treated as direct messages by the database, regardless of their declared kind.
  • Removing people cannot create a forbidden pair either: if members leave a non-exempt conversation until only two remain, the database re-checks that remaining pair and blocks the removal if those two are not allowed a private conversation.

Moderated rooms

  • Sanctioned rooms — org-wide, group, staff, and event chats set up under staff permission — are moderated spaces. Members with the moderation permission can see any conversation in their organization, so reported content can always be reviewed and acted on.
  • Moderators can pin, soft-delete, and remove messages; deletions leave the thread legible rather than silently rewriting history.

Reporting

  • Any member can report a message. Reports go into a moderation queue visible to the organization's moderators, with a status trail (open, reviewing, actioned, dismissed).
  • Reports are never deleted — only resolved — so there is always a record of what was raised and what was done about it.

Guardian links

  • Organizations link parents and guardians to their own students in the database. A guardian sees their own child's information — including form responses such as medical forms — and no other student's.
  • A student may have several linked guardians, and a guardian several students; the link is explicit, per-student, and set by the organization.

Audit log

  • Sensitive administrative actions — role changes, permission-relevant changes, and similar — are written to a per-organization audit log with the acting user and timestamp, so an organization can answer "who changed this, and when."

What organizations must still do

  • Mark minors as minors when adding members — the pair rules key off that flag.
  • Grant staff roles only to vetted, authorized adults, and review the role list periodically.
  • Decide deliberately whether to enable student_dms or student_staff_dms, consistent with the organization's own policies. Cadence ships them off.
  • Link guardians to students so families see what they should.
  • Watch the report queue and act on it. Cadence surfaces reports; the organization's moderators resolve them.
  • Follow the organization's own legal and policy obligations for supervising communication with minors. Cadence's mechanics support common two-adult and no-private-contact policies but do not replace them.

Honest limits

  • Cadence cannot stop people from communicating outside Cadence.
  • Cadence relies on the organization to say who is a minor and who is staff; the database enforces the rules over what the organization has declared.
  • Cadence does not claim compliance with any law governing minors' data or online safety. The design intent — data minimization, guardian involvement, organization-controlled access enforced by database permissions — is described here plainly, and formal legal review of these mechanics is a required step before launch and before any organization is charged.

Reporting a safety concern to Cadence

Safety concerns about the platform itself — a rule not working as described here, or conduct an organization will not address — can be sent to the contact address published on the Cadence site. If someone is in danger, contact local emergency services first.
